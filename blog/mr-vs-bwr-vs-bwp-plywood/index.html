MR vs BWR vs BWP plywood: a practical buying guide

Plywood grade should follow the moisture exposure and use of the space. MR, BWR and BWP are not simply better, best and premium versions of the same board.

For a useful specification, begin with the application. A dry wardrobe, a kitchen cabinet near a sink and a utility-area shutter need different levels of moisture resistance.

MR plywood

MR means Moisture Resistant. MR plywood is commonly considered for dry interior applications such as furniture bodies, wardrobes and general indoor work where direct water exposure is limited.

BWR plywood

BWR means Boiling Water Resistant. It is selected when the project needs more moisture resistance than a typical dry interior application, while exposure remains controlled and construction details protect the board.

BWP plywood

BWP means Boiling Water Proof. It is generally discussed for demanding moisture conditions, including wet-prone kitchen and utility applications. Always confirm the product specification and applicable standard before ordering.

How to choose between the three

GradeThink about it forFirst question
MRDry furniture and interior workWill the board remain protected from direct moisture?
BWRModerate or controlled moisture exposureDoes the design need extra resistance without constant wet exposure?
BWPHigher or repeated moisture exposureCould the board face water, humidity or wet-area conditions?

Do not specify grade alone

Include thickness, sheet size, application, finish, quantity and delivery location in the specification. Edges, laminates, sealants, joinery and installation also influence the service life of the finished work.

  • Identify the room and moisture exposure.
  • Choose the thickness required by the design.
  • Ask for the current datasheet and certification details.
  • Confirm availability before finalizing quantities.
