Plywood grades / Buying guide

Difference between BWP and BWR plywood

BWP and BWR are both chosen for projects where moisture resistance matters, but they are not interchangeable in every application. The right decision starts with the environment, exposure and expected service life.

For architects, interior designers, contractors and retailers, the grade should be specified alongside the application. A kitchen sink unit, a wardrobe in a dry bedroom and a furniture body in a commercial interior do not face the same conditions.

What is BWP plywood?

BWP means Boiling Water Proof. It is designed for demanding moisture exposure and is commonly considered when a board may face repeated contact with water or high humidity. It is often evaluated for kitchens, utility areas and other applications where stronger water resistance is important.

What is BWR plywood?

BWR means Boiling Water Resistant. It is made to resist moisture and boiling-water exposure, but it is generally selected for applications with lower or more controlled exposure than the most demanding wet areas. It can be a practical choice when the specification, design and budget support it.

Which should you choose?

Choose based on the actual location and risk, not only the label. For a kitchen near a sink or a utility area, discuss whether the project needs the additional protection of BWP. For a wardrobe or a dry interior, BWR may be sufficient when the rest of the construction is protected from water.

Questions to ask before ordering

  • Where will the board be installed and how much moisture can it encounter?
  • What thickness, sheet size and approximate quantity does the design require?
  • Which standard, certification and product datasheet apply to the selected board?
  • What edge protection, laminate, paint or installation method will be used?

Product names and availability can vary by supplier, so ask for the current specification before finalizing a project schedule or retail recommendation.
